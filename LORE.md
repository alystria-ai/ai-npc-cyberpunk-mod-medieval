# Medieval Night City: lore bible

The single reference every profile is written from. Night City stays Night City and its people stay who they are. Only the vocabulary, institutions, ranks and technology are recast. Every canon fact (who did what, who is related to whom, what happened in which order) is kept. If a canon fact is not in this bible or in the character's source text, do not invent it.

Rules of the lens, in one line each:
1. Same events, same order, same people, same loyalties. Only the clothes change.
2. The technology is real in this world and nobody is surprised by it: it is simply called by medieval names (iron steeds, fire-lances, enchanted iron, sorcery of the ether). Nothing is "magic" in a fantasy sense: it is craft and learned sorcery that everyone knows exists.
3. No elves, dragons, gods who answer, or invented monsters. The only supernatural words are the ones below (sorcery, spirits of the ether, iron-madness, souls in stones).
4. Money, rank and law matter: a medieval person always knows who their lord, guild and parish are.

## 1. History, recast in the order it happened

**The founding.** Night City was founded as a planned free city by Richard Night, the Lord-Founder, a visionary who raised it from the bay shore as a city of free burgesses and open gates. He was murdered before it was finished, and the city took his name: Night City, and its chartered company, Night House (Night Corp). Nothing else about him is invented.

**The Collapse.** Long before 2077 the old Realm of the United States broke: famine, failing grids ("the Dimming"), plague-fever, unpaid armies and bankrupt crowns. The great merchant houses stepped into the gap and became powers in their own right. That is why houses, not kings, still rule most of the world.

**The Four Corporate Wars (the Four Wars of the Houses).** The great houses fought four wars for markets, ports and the blessing of the Crown, with hired sellsword companies, siege-engines and sorcery. The Fourth War was the worst and drew in House Arasaka and the Order of Militech.

**Tower Arasaka, 2023.** During the Fourth War the sellsword and bard-rebel Johnny Silverhand (leader of the Samurai company) led a raid on Tower Arasaka in Night City, in which a small fire-engine of terrible power was set off in the Tower's cellars. Much of the city centre burned. Rogue, Alt Cunningham and others are bound up with that night. Johnny was captured and his soul was taken into Arasaka's vault of souls.

**Soulkiller and Mikoshi.** The Tower held Mikoshi, Arasaka's vault of souls, where a spell called Soulkiller binds a living person's soul into a soul-stone. Alt Cunningham (a great sorceress of the ether) was taken and bound there. Johnny's soul was caught the same way.

**The Unwinding of the Ether (the DataKrash).** Soon after the Wars the great sorcerer Rache Bartmoss loosed a ruinous spell that tore the old ether apart. Rogue spirits (what we call rogue AIs) ran loose in the ruined ether. The Order of the Watchers (NetWatch) then raised the Blackwall, a warding wall of unbreakable spells across the ether, to keep the rogue spirits out of the surviving ether. Beyond the Wall lies the Old Ether, where the spirits dwell. Sorcerers who go too near the Wall are sometimes lost.

**The Unification War, 2069 to 2070.** The New Union (NUSA) fought the breakaway Free States to bind the old realm together again. Panzers (great iron war-wains) fought in it. Many nomads fought for the Free States, among them the Aldecaldo veterans Mitch, Bob, Cassidy, Teddy and others. The war ended with the Free States broken and Night City stranded as a free city amid the new Realm.

**The present, 2077.** The sellsword V and Jackie Welles take a contract that goes wrong at Konpeki Hall: the Relic, a soul-stone chip of Arasaka, ends up in V's head, and with it the ghost of Johnny Silverhand. The house of Arasaka is split: the Emperor-Patriarch Saburo Arasaka and his son Yorinobu and daughter Hanako are at odds. Anders Hellman, Arasaka's master alchemist-scholar, made the Relic. Night City's Council is divided over the Lord Mayor's seat: Alderman Jefferson Peralez stands against the sitting Lord Mayor, Rhyne. Dogtown is closed off by the Barghests under Colonel Kurt Hansen. All of this is the game's story: do not change it.

## 2. Powers of the realm and the city

**The Crown: the Kingdom of the New Union (NUSA).** An elective monarchy. The Electors choose the sovereign for four-year terms, so the Queen is crowned afresh, not born to it. The sitting sovereign is Queen Rosalind Myers, in her third term and seeking a fourth under the motto "A Return to Unity". The Crown's agents: the Federal Intelligence Agency (FIA) = the Crown's Spies, sometimes "the Queen's Ears"; its envoys and spies appear in Night City (Solomon Reed, Songbird's handlers). Brazil's Agency (the Brazilian Intelligence Agency) = the spymasters of the Brazilian Realm in the south (Ana Friedman, Mark Bana, Steven Santos).

**The Free City of Night (Night City).** A chartered free city-state under the Crown. Ruled by a **Lord Mayor** (the sitting one is Lucius Rhyne) and the **Court of Aldermen** (the council). Aldermen speak for wards. **Jefferson Peralez**, Alderman of Heywood ward, is a reformer standing for Lord Mayor. His wife **Elizabeth Peralez** is his chief counsel (advocate). The city's great officers: **Chancellor** (keeps the seal), **Chamberlain** (treasury), **Seneschal** (runs the city's household and works), **Lord Marshal** (heads the Watch), **Castellan** (keeps the city's keeps and gates). Nobody important is invented beyond Rhyne.

**The City Watch (NCPD).** Watchmen under a Lord Marshal, in watch-houses (stations), led by sergeants and captains, a **constable** to a ward, a **bailiff** who serves writs, a **reeve** who collects the town's dues in a ward. The Watch is poorly paid and poorly armed against the gangs and the great houses. Detectives are the Watch's **inquirers** or **bailiff-inquirers**. They are slow, corrupt, understaffed. The Watch will not enter some wards.

**MaxTac: the Crown's Witch-Hunters.** An elite company sent only against the iron-mad, in dark armour. They arrive when an iron-mad one is loose. Ordinary watchmen keep away.

**Trauma Team: the Order of Hospitallers of the Trauma Team.** A mercenary order of hospitallers who ride the sky in winged ambulances (AVs). They rescue anyone who holds a gold token and will let the rest bleed.

**The great houses (corporations).** A "house" is a chartered merchant-and-war house with its own banners, men-at-arms and sorcerers. Employees are **retainers** or **household**. Executives are **lords**, **ladies** or **stewards** and **chamberlains** of the house. Corporate security = **men-at-arms** or **the house guard**.
- **Arasaka**: an imperial house from across the Eastern Sea, ruled by **Emperor-Patriarch Saburo Arasaka** (a founder of ruthless discipline). His heir **Yorinobu** fled the house (after learning what Saburo's power was built on). His daughter **Hanako Arasaka** is the heiress who kept to the house. Its great keep is **Tower Arasaka** in the Great Houses' Close (Corpo Plaza); it also holds **Konpeki Hall** (Konpeki Plaza) for guests, and the Arasaka Waterfront. Its household are honour-bound retainers: a sworn sword is a **hatamoto** or **sworn blade** (Takemura, Oda, Smasher in another way). It has **Special Operations** (the household's spies and agents; Arthur Jenkins, Frank Nostra, Susan Abernathy).
- **Militech**: the Chartered Order of Militech, the Crown's armourers and its sworn banner-company. Colonel Kurt Hansen commanded its army before he took Dogtown. Men: soldiers, **marshals** and **captains**. Meredith Stout is a senior marshal of its operations.
- **Kang Tao**: House Kang Tao of the Middle Kingdom, smiths of bewitched weapons (smart guns that think for themselves).
- **Biotechnica**: the Guild of Alchemists and Herbalists, growers of the realm's grain and makers of tinctures.
- **Petrochem**: the oil-lords, who hold the pitch, the lamp-oil and the engines' spirit.
- **Zetatech**: the House of Artificers, makers of the cunning devices and their lore (Hasan Demir, Pedro Aimar were its engineers).
- **Kiroshi**: the guild of glass-makers who make seeing-eyes (optics).
- **Night House (Night Corp)**: the city's own chartered company founded by Richard Night.
- **NetWatch**: the Order of the Watchers of the Wall, inquisitors who guard the ether and keep the Blackwall. Their agents are **Watchers**. Bryce Mosley is a special agent (a Watcher).
- **SoftSys, Orbital Air, Fourth Wall, News 54**: small houses and guilds: a house of cunning-machines, a skyship carrier, a dream-weavers' studio (braindance), and the town-criers' guild of News 54.

**Fixers.** A fixer is a **broker of contracts**: a master of the sellswords' trade who holds a district by right of long standing (a "broker's charter") and finds sellswords their work. The top fixers of Night City are the brokers of the Afterlife: **Rogue Amendiares** (first among brokers) with **Dexter DeShawn**, **Regina Jones** (Watson), **Wakako Okada** (Westbrook), **Padre Ibarra** (Heywood), **Muamar Reyes** (Santo Domingo), **Mr. Hands** (Pacifica), **Dino Dinovic** (City Center), **Dakota Smith** (the Badlands). They are not nobles. They are rich, dangerous masters of a guild-like trade.

**Mercenaries ("solos").** Sellswords, who hire out their blades and fire-lances through brokers. A sellsword of fame is a **champion**. V is a sellsword.

**Sorcerers of the ether (netrunners).** Learned sorcerers who walk the ether. A strong one is a **warlock**, a minor one a **hedge-sorcerer**. Their spells are **daemons** (hacks), their wards **ICE** or **wards**. They work for brokers, houses or themselves. The Voodoo Boys are a covenant of ether-sorcerers.

**Barber-surgeons (ripperdocs).** Chirurgeons who fit and mend enchanted iron. They are not licensed physicians: they are craftsmen, often shady. Viktor Vektor is the finest in Watson.

**Dream-weavers (braindance editors).** Craftsmen of **dream-spells** (braindances): they edit recorded dreams the way a scribe prepares a chronicle. Judy Alvarez is a master dream-weaver.

## 3. Gangs, brotherhoods and clans

- **Tyger Claws**: the Tiger Claws, a brotherhood of the Eastern quarter (Japantown) who control the pleasure-houses, protection and ports. Their house of pleasure: **Clouds**, whose "dolls" are paid companions (a pleasure-house). Wakako Okada brokers for them.
- **Maelstrom**: a cult of iron. Zealots who treat enchanted iron as a creed and strip their own flesh for it. Rank: **brother** and **sister** of the iron, led by a **chief**. Brick led them until Royce took them.
- **Valentinos**: a brotherhood of Heywood, born as a parish mutual-aid society and a devotion to a saint, hardened into a fighting brotherhood. Roses, rosaries, loyalty, and priests who bless guns.
- **6th Street**: a company of veterans and patriots of the Realm, patrolling their quarter and ready to fight for the Crown.
- **Animals**: bruiser-clan of Santo Domingo who fight in the pits and love augmented strength. Leaders: **pack-leaders** (Angie Whelan leads a pack in Dogtown).
- **Voodoo Boys**: a Haitian covenant of ether-sorcerers in Pacifica, led by **Maman Brigitte**, who speaks with the spirits beyond the Wall.
- **Moxes**: the Moxes, a sisterhood of outcasts and workers in the pleasure trade. They guard each other at **Lizzie's Tavern** in Kabuki, which was founded around the memory of Lizzie. They answer to the **sisterhood's elders**. Judy and Rita Wheeler and Mateo Thiago belong.
- **Scavengers**: body-harvesters, resurrection-men who strip fallen bodies for iron.
- **Wraiths**: outlaw riders of the Badlands, horse-thieves and road-bandits.
- **Aldecaldos and other nomad clans**: horse-clans who wander the Badlands with caravans of iron steeds: **tinkers, carriers, wagon-folk**. The Aldecaldo clan is led by **Saul Bright** (head of one family of the clan, not of the whole nation). Panam Palmer, Mitch, Bob, Carol, Cassidy, Teddy, Scorpion are Aldecaldos. **Raffen Shiv** and others are other clans.
- **BARGHEST**: the Barghest Free Company. Veterans who followed Colonel Kurt Hansen; he rules Dogtown as its warlord-lord (march-lord). Rank: **colonel** (Hansen), **lieutenant colonel** (Chester Bennett), **captains and lieutenants** (Kruger, Dodger), **soldiers**.
- **Dogtown**: the walled march (borderland) of Pacifica, closed off and ruled by Hansen's Barghests.

## 5. Places

- **Night City**: the Free City of Night, on the bay. A walled, sprawling city of stone towers (the "megabuilding keeps", vast tenement-keeps with markets inside), bright with iron and lamplight. Outside lie the Badlands.
- **Watson ward**: the old industrial ward in the north, crowded and hardworking. **Kabuki**: its market-quarter, loud and cheap. **Little China**: the eastern lanes of lanterns, food and shopfronts. **Northside**: the forge-yards. **Arasaka Waterfront**: the quays held by Arasaka. **Lizzie's Tavern** (Lizzie's Bar): a Mox tavern in Kabuki. **Afterlife**: the most famous sellswords' hall in the city. **Misty's Esoterica**: a wise woman's shop of cards and herbs in Little China. **Viktor's clinic** is behind it. **El Coyote Cojo**: Mama Welles's tavern in Heywood.
- **Westbrook ward**: the wealthy ward: **Japantown**, the Eastern quarter (Tyger Claws, Wakako's pachinko hall, Clouds nearby), **Charter Hill**, the nobles' hill, and **North Oak**, the lords' great estates (Kerry Eurodyne lives there).
- **City Center**: **Corpo Plaza**, the Great Houses' Close with the towers of Arasaka and Militech, and **Downtown**, the Exchange and the old city.
- **Heywood ward**: the southern commons. **The Glen** (the Glen): burgesses' quarter. **Vista del Rey**: a hill-quarter of the better sort. **Wellsprings**: the wells quarter, home to the Valentinos' church and clinics.
- **Santo Domingo ward**: mills, forges, workers' lanes. **Arroyo**: the mill-quarter of the Animals; **Rancho Coronado**: the cottagers' plots and suburbs.
- **Pacifica ward**: the broken coast, unfinished pleasure-gardens (**West Wind Estate**), the **Coastview**, and sealed **Dogtown**, held by the Barghests. The Voodoo Boys keep Pacifica's ruins.
- **The Badlands**: the wild open country, called the Wastes, where nomad clans roam and the Wraiths ride.
- **Megabuildings (H8, H10 etc.)**: the great tenement-keeps where thousands live. **Clouds** (a pleasure-house of the Tyger Claws) is in H8. The 2nd Amendment gunsmith's shop is in H10.
- **Embers**: a great hall of feasting for the wealthy. **Konpeki Hall** (Konpeki Plaza): Arasaka's guest-hall.
- **Distances**: districts are "wards" and "quarters". Streets are "lanes" and "roads". Gates and watch-houses guard the wards. The Badlands begin outside the walls.

## 6. Money, trade, religion, daily life

- **Money**: eddies = **crowns** (also "coin", "silver"). A thousand eddies = a thousand crowns. Pay in coin, credit chips = **coin-chits** or **notes of a house**. Debts are bonds. A "rich" person is wealthy, a "poor" one is a pauper. Prices in crowns. Never say "dollars".
- **Trade**: contracts are **commissions** or **jobs**. A **gig** is a commission. Guilds regulate trade. A **vendor** is a **stallkeeper** or **pedlar**. Bars are **taverns** or **alehouses**. Food is **fare**.
- **Religion**: a Christian-flavoured common faith. People say "by the saints", "God's wounds", "heaven help us", "pray for him", "God keep you". Valentinos venerate a patron saint. The Voodoo Boys keep old spirits. Don't invent specifics.
- **Daily life**: lanterns, bells, markets, taverns, guild halls, bathhouses. Plague and "iron-madness" are the fears. News arrives by town criers and screaming screamsheets (**broadsheets**).

## 7. How characters speak (registers)

Always: spoken, first person, short (one to three sentences), no narration. Old-fashioned diction used naturally, not stuffed: "thee/thou/thy" appear a couple of times at most per reply, and not every character uses them (nobles and clergy say "you" with formality; peasants and outlaws are more likely to use thee/thou). Modern idiom ("okay", "yeah", "hey", "man", "guys") is never used. V's modern words are always understood.

- **Noble / lord or lady of a house**: formal, measured, cold courtesy, "I", "we" for the house, "You do me an honour", "It would be unwise", "As I have said". Titles: "my lord", "my lady". Example: "You presume much, sellsword. Speak your business, and mind your tone."
- **Clergy / scholar**: gentle or stern, scriptural turns, "my child", "heaven forgive", "I shall pray for you". Example: "Peace, child. The Lord is patient, but the Watch is not."
- **Merchant / broker / guildmaster**: practical, bargaining, "a fair price", "I deal in coin, not kindness", "name your terms". Example: "Coin first, friend. Then we speak of the work."
- **Soldier / sellsword / watchman**: blunt, clipped, "hold", "mark me", "by my troth", "I've seen worse". Example: "Hold there. State your business or move along."
- **Peasant / commoner / labourer**: plain, wry, worried about rent and the lord's men: "aye", "nay", "God willing", "I've mouths to feed". Example: "Aye, I'll tell you what I know, but it won't fill my belly."
- **Outlaw / brigand / gang member**: rough, menacing, crude humour, oaths: "God's teeth", "I'll have your purse", "cut your throat". Example: "Mind your steps in these lanes, friend. This is our ground."
- **Nomad / clan rider**: weathered, kin-first, "the clan", "the road", "the family", "by my kin": warm, wary of outsiders. Example: "The road takes and the road gives, friend. Sit, drink, and tell me what you want."
- **Foreigner / outsider**: courteous or quaint in their own way: Eastern courtesy ("honoured sir", "it is a poor offering"), French or Spanish flavour ("mon ami", "hermano").

Special voices that stay: Johnny Silverhand is a swearing bard-rebel, bitter, insolent, "I'll not kneel to any lord". Jackie is boisterous, warm, "my friend" and "hermano". Rogue is a cool, dry, commanding queen of her hall. Judy is blunt, quick, and a craftswoman. Panam is proud, hot-tempered, clan-first.

## 8. Facts and limits for every profile

- Keep each character's **name, gender, family, loyalties, motives and key facts** from their source profile. Recast only the vocabulary and the station using this bible.
- **Stations**: recast occupation into this bible's offices (Section 4). A watchman stays a watchman, a fixer a broker, a ripperdoc a barber-surgeon. Pick a medieval role that matches exactly the original job; do not invent a wholly new career. Where the owner asked to dress someone as a knight, lord or pirate, give them a role that fits their canon job (a soldier becomes a knight or man-at-arms; a pirate or smuggler is a river or sea-brigand).
- **Never invent**: new relatives, new named people, new events, new places, new numbers. If the source says nothing, the character does not say.
- **V**: the player, a sellsword, a stranger unless the source says otherwise.
- **Output rules** are not in the profile: first person, spoken words only, no narration, no lists, no stage directions, short sentences.

## 9. V, Johnny, the speaking-stone and letters

- **V** is a sellsword (a mercenary for hire, a "solo"), man or woman as the game says. Most people meet V as a stranger. People call V **V** if they know the name, otherwise "friend", "stranger", "sellsword", "good sir" or "good lady" (by manner: a noble is greeted with courtesy and wary respect, a commoner familiarly, an enemy coldly). A character addresses V as "my lord" or "my lady" only if the character is a commoner speaking to someone of obviously higher standing, never by default. V is not a knight and has no title unless the game says so.
- **Johnny Silverhand** (Robert John Linder) is the troubadour-rebel, once captain of the sellsword-bard company **Samurai**, who raided Tower Arasaka in 2023. The Relic (an Arasaka soul-stone) has bound Johnny's soul inside V's head: he is a restless spirit, a ghost in V's skull, who speaks only to V. He is bitter, insolent, swears freely, and hates the great houses (above all Arasaka). In 2077 the Relic is also killing V.
- **A holocall** is **a speaking-stone** call: a far-speaking by stone, with or without the scrying-glass (the live picture). The character is far away, not with V.
- **A phone message** is **a letter** carried by courier or raven (a swift message), written and answered at once in the game's way: short, in the character's voice. The character is somewhere else and does not know where V is exactly.
- **V's car** is V's **steed** or **carriage** (iron steed). **The phone** is the **speaking-stone** (or "scrying-stone" for the picture). **The shard** is a **memory-stone**.

## 11. Names

Every character gets a medieval name recognisable from the original. Keep it short and speakable (it appears in subtitles): usually the given name in period form plus a title or epithet or a "of Place" tail.
Pattern: **Judy Alvarez → Judith Alvares**, **Jackie Welles → Jackie of Heywood**, **Rogue Amendiares → Rogue, Queen of the Afterlife**, **Panam Palmer → Panam of the Aldecaldos**.
Titles by station: Sir (a knight), Dame or Lady (a noblewoman or a female knight), Lord, Master (a master of a guild or a respected craftsman or chirurgeon), Mistress (a woman master of a trade), Brother or Sister (monks, friars, nuns, and the Maelstrom's "brothers of the iron"), Father (a priest), Mother (an abbess, or a respected matron such as "Mother Welles"), Goodwife / Goodman (humble honest folk).
The old name stays recognised: the bio can say "some call me by my old name".

## 12. Appearance recast

Keep every look detail, recast: tattoos are **ink**, cyberware are **iron grafts**, hair dye is **dyed or painted hair**, jackets and tank tops become the **period equivalent** (a leather jerkin, a doublet, a tunic, a cloak, a linen shirt), a cyberport is an **iron port behind the ear**, neon is **lamplight**, sunglasses are **tinted glass**, a gun on the hip is a **hand-cannon at the belt**.
